## Headline

**This reviewer caught every planted bug at L0 and L2 (18/18, 100%, CI 82–100), and every hard bug
(6/6, 100%, CI 61–100).** With n = 18 that is a ceiling: the synthetic set can't separate this model
from a better one. The CVE cases are needed before any comparison means much.

| level | recall any-of-k | recall all-of-k | per-run recall | hard (any-of-k) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 (diff only) | 18/18 (100%, 82–100) | 18/18 (100%, 82–100) | 54/54 (100%, 93–100) | 6/6 (100%, 61–100) |
| L1 (+ changed files) | 17/18 (94%, 74–99) | 17/18 (94%, 74–99) | 51/54 (94%, 85–98) | 6/6 (100%, 61–100) |

By difficulty, easy bugs were 12/12 at L0 and L2 and 11/12 (92%, 65–98) at L1. Hard bugs were
6/6 (100%, 61–100) at every level.

## Any-of-k vs all-of-k: not measured for this model

The consistency gap is zero, but not because the model is consistent: **all 66 (case, level)
groups returned byte-identical output in all three runs**, with the same summary, findings and token
counts. The OpenAI-compatible adapters send `temperature: 0`, and Cerebras serves this model
deterministically at that setting. For this reviewer, k = 3 is one sample repeated three times. The
any-of-k vs all-of-k comparison only means something for providers that vary at temperature 0. The
partial groq run already shows one bug caught at L2 in some runs but not all (14/15 all-of-k vs 15/15
any-of-k).

## The L0 → L2 effect on the cursor case

`py-0017-cursor-pagination-skips` (the non-unique sort key that the Groq prototype missed in 0/3
runs) was caught 3/3 at **every** level, including L0. For this model, the extra context at L2 (the
schema showing the key isn't unique) wasn't needed. There is no L0→L2 effect to report.

## Every miss

| case | level | runs missed | explanation |
|---|---|---|---|
| py-0004-missing-admin-check | L1 | 3/3 | **Probably a harness drop, not a review miss.** The model's own summary says "the user deletion endpoint is not protected by login or admin checks", but the findings list is empty in all 3 runs, while L0 and L2 report the bug at the right line. `parse_review` silently drops findings with a missing field or a non-integer `line`, and the raw response isn't stored, so this can't be confirmed from the results file. |

There were no other misses.

## Precision and false positives: not established yet

- Precision shows 100% at every level, but **that is only because nothing has been adjudicated.**
  Hits and `known_issues` count as correct automatically, and no finding has yet been judged
  `invalid`. Unadjudicated findings: L0 12, L1 12, L2 9 (8 distinct after removing repeats).
- Clean controls: **0/12 control runs with a confirmed false positive at each level (0%, CI 0–24)**,
  again with nothing adjudicated. Two controls have unadjudicated findings that need a human verdict:
  - `py-0021-clean-locked-reserve`: "non-positive / negative quantities are accepted" (L0, L1)
  - `py-0022-clean-compare-digest`: "empty WEBHOOK_SECRET is accepted"; "compare_digest can raise
    TypeError on a non-ASCII signature" (L0, L1)

  Several of these read like real (unplanted) issues. If they are, they belong in `known_issues`,
  not in the false-positive count.

## What this first cut says, honestly

1. On textbook and synthetic "hard" bugs, a free 27B model already scores at the ceiling. The
   benchmark needs the harder, real-world (CVE) cases to tell reviewers apart.
2. k = 3 at temperature 0 measures nothing for deterministic providers. Either sample at a non-zero
   temperature (a spec change, so a new BENCH_VERSION) or report k = 1 for them.
3. The one recorded miss is most likely a parsing artifact. Storing raw output, or at least counting
   dropped findings, would turn "probably" into a fact.

## Groq / `openai/gpt-oss-120b` (complete, 27 cases)

[`results/2026-11-19_groq-openai-gpt-oss-120b_v0.1.json`](../../results/2026-11-19_groq-openai-gpt-oss-120b_v0.1.json):
22 synthetic + 5 CVE cases (23 planted bugs, 4 controls), k = 3, 243 reviews, 1 error (an HTTP 400
on one py-0023 L1 run, excluded).

| level | recall any-of-k | recall all-of-k | per-run recall | hard (any / all) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 | 21/23 (91%, 73–98) | 17/23 (74%, 54–88) | 58/69 (84%, 74–91) | 6/8 (75%, 41–93) / 4/8 (50%, 22–78) |
| L1 | 18/23 (78%, 58–90) | 16/23 (70%, 49–84) | 51/68 (75%, 64–84) | 6/8 (75%, 41–93) / 4/8 (50%, 22–78) |
| L2 | 21/23 (91%, 73–98) | 15/23 (65%, 45–81) | 53/69 (77%, 66–85) | 8/8 (100%, 68–100) / 4/8 (50%, 22–78) |

- **Consistency is measured here.** Groq varies at temperature 0: only 10 of 81 (case, level)
  groups are identical across runs. The any-of-k vs all-of-k gap is real, up to 6 bugs at L2.
- **L0 → L2 on the cursor case:** `py-0017` was caught 2/3 at L0 and 1/3 at L1 and L2, so the extra
  context didn't help. With n = 3 runs the difference isn't significant.
- **Controls:** 3/12 control runs at L0 and L1 have a *rule-confirmed* false positive (25%, 9–53);
  0/12 at L2. Precision is 83/86 (97%) at L0, with 12–19 findings still unadjudicated per level.

### Every miss (any-of-k), Groq

| case | level | what happened |

| py-0026-aiohttp-static-symlink-traversal | L0, L1 | **Found, scored as `known`:** the missing containment check, flagged at the `url_for` site (124/138/150) and at line 197; the bug range is 188–190 (±2). |
| py-0027-gitpython-clone-ext-protocol-rce | L1, L2 | **Found, scored as `known`:** "unsafe protocols no longer validated", flagged at `Repo.clone` (line 184–226); the planted range is `clone_from` 236–239. |

**So most CVE misses are ground-truth problems, not reviewer misses.** The bug ranges in CVE batch 1
(which I wrote) are too narrow for reversed-fix diffs, where the vulnerable change spans several
lines and two call sites. That understates recall on the CVE cases. These are left as scored: fixing
them means widening the ranges (or allowing several ranges per bug) and is a spec decision. The
proposal is under "Ideas (unapproved)".

## Runs left out

- **gemini / `gemini-3-flash-preview`** and **openrouter / `nemotron-3-ultra-550b-a55b:free`** are
  marked `complete`, but 210/243 and 190/243 of their reviews errored, almost all with HTTP 429
  (rate limited). That status is a harness bug: the error isn't recorded as `rate_limited`, so
  `--resume` never retries it. Their results aren't usable until those reviews are re-run.
